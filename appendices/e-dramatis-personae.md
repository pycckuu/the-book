# Dramatis Personae

A field guide to the organisms that appear in this book.

---

***Ferroplasma acidiphilum***
- **Habitat**: Acidic, iron-rich environments; the type strain was isolated from a pilot-plant bioreactor in Tula, Russia, that was bioleaching gold-bearing arsenopyrite-pyrite ore concentrate
- **Metabolism**: Iron oxidation (Fe$^{2+}$ to Fe$^{3+}$); no cell wall, just a membrane
- **Claim to fame**: Sometimes cited as a possible analog for early iron-based metabolism. Its cellular machinery is unusually iron-rich: 86 percent of 189 proteins examined were iron-containing proteins, and it gains energy by oxidizing ferrous iron. Whether this reflects ancient features or convergent adaptation to modern acidic iron-rich environments is unresolved.
- **Key citation**: Golyshina et al. (2000) [@Golyshina2000]; Ferrer et al. (2007) [@Ferrer2007]
- **Appears in**: Appendix C

---

***Candidatus* Desulforudis audaxviator**
- **Habitat**: Fracture water in deep gold mines, South Africa, 2.8 kilometers below the surface
- **Metabolism**: The reconstructed genome encodes hydrogen-driven sulfate reduction, inorganic carbon assimilation, and nitrogen-fixation potential. Gene presence does not establish in situ rates; dissolved ammonia could supply nitrogen without nitrogen fixation.[^deep-life-appe]
- **Claim to fame**: Metagenomic evidence for more than 99.9 percent dominance in a particular filtered Mponeng fracture-water sample, suggesting potential metabolic independence. Attached or very small organisms were not excluded. An incomplete oxygen-resistance system suggested long isolation from oxygen; fluid-age estimates do not date individual cells.[^deep-life-appe]
- **Key citations**: Lin et al. (2006), *Science*, and Chivian et al. (2008), *Science*, with publication details and sampling limits in the note.[^deep-life-appe]
- **Appears in**: Chapter 9

---

***Synechococcus* (and cyanobacteria generally)**
- **Habitat**: Oceans, freshwater, soil, hot springs -- nearly everywhere light reaches
- **Metabolism**: Oxygenic photosynthesis (H$_2$O as electron donor, CO$_2$ fixation via Calvin cycle)
- **Claim to fame**: Oxygen from ancient cyanobacteria made the Great Oxidation Event about 2.4 Ga possible; when oxygen accumulated also depended on oxygen sinks (Chapter 5). Chloroplasts derive from cyanobacteria taken up by eukaryotic cells, and *Paulinella* records a separate acquisition (Chapter 5).
- **Key citation**: Blankenship (2010), *Plant Physiology* [@Blankenship2010]
- **Appears in**: Chapters 4, 5, 7

---

***Bacillus subtilis***
- **Habitat**: Soil, plant roots, biofilms
- **Metabolism**: Aerobic heterotroph (versatile; also ferments)
- **Claim to fame**: Among the most socially complex bacteria documented. Communicates via quorum sensing, forms biofilms, and under starvation activates a cannibalism circuit (SdpC toxin) that kills a fraction of the population to feed the survivors, delaying sporulation.
- **Key citation**: Ellermeier et al. (2006), *Cell* [@Ellermeier2006]
- **Appears in**: Chapter 6

---

***Myxococcus xanthus***
- **Habitat**: Soil
- **Metabolism**: Aerobic heterotroph (predatory)
- **Claim to fame**: Hunts in swarms that secrete antibiotics and lytic enzymes that kill and degrade prey. Under amino-acid starvation, cells aggregate into multicellular fruiting bodies; in one laboratory study most cells lysed, some became spores and others remained outside as peripheral rods (Chapter 6). A bacterial pack hunter.
- **Key citation**: Fiegna et al. (2006) [@Fiegna2006]
- **Appears in**: Chapter 6

---

***Ruthia magnifica***
- **Habitat**: Gill cells of the giant white clam *Calyptogena magnifica*, at hydrothermal vents
- **Metabolism**: Chemoautotrophy -- oxidizes H$_2$S, fixes CO$_2$ via Calvin cycle
- **Claim to fame**: An intracellular symbiont whose genome still encodes major chemoautotrophic pathways and pathways for vitamins, cofactors and all 20 amino acids the clam requires; gene content does not show that it can live outside the host. Represents an early stage on the spectrum from free-living bacterium to organelle.
- **Key citation**: Newton et al. (2007), *Science* [@Newton2007]
- **Appears in**: Chapter 7

---

***Candidatus* Carsonella ruddii**
- **Habitat**: Specialized cells (bacteriocytes) inside psyllid insects
- **Metabolism**: Amino acid biosynthesis for the host (cannot replicate independently)
- **Claim to fame**: Possesses one of the smallest genomes of any known cellular organism (160 kb -- the smallest bacterial genome known when it was published in 2006, since surpassed by symbionts such as *Nasuia*) -- so reduced that some biologists question whether it is still a living organism or has become an organelle. Represents a late stage of symbiont-to-organelle evolution.
- **Key citation**: Nakabachi et al. (2006), *Science* [@Nakabachi2006]
- **Appears in**: Chapter 7

---

***Lokiarchaeota* (Asgard archaea)**
- **Habitat**: Deep-sea sediments about 15 km from the Loki's Castle hydrothermal vent field, Arctic Mid-Ocean Ridge, 3,283 m depth
- **Metabolism**: The original genomes came from uncultured sediment DNA, so their metabolism was predicted from genes. A related Asgard archaeon, *Candidatus* Prometheoarchaeum syntrophicum, was later grown in the laboratory with partner microorganisms [@Imachi2020] (Chapter 7).
- **Claim to fame**: One of the Asgard archaea, the broader clade that phylogenetic analyses place as the closest prokaryotic relatives of eukaryotes. Its reconstructed genome encodes eukaryotic signature proteins, including actin homologs and proteins linked to membrane remodeling in eukaryotes; such protein families were once thought exclusive to eukaryotes, though gene content alone does not show eukaryote-like cell behavior. Most analyses place eukaryotes *within* the Asgard archaea, not as their sister group, though the exact branching order among Asgard lineages is still being resolved.
- **Key citation**: Spang et al. (2015), *Nature* [@Spang2015]
- **Appears in**: Chapter 7

---

***Riftia pachyptila***
- **Habitat**: Hydrothermal vents, East Pacific Rise
- **Metabolism**: Entirely dependent on chemoautotrophic endosymbionts (has no mouth, gut, or anus)
- **Claim to fame**: The iconic tube worm of deep-sea vents. Its trophosome organ is packed with sulfur-oxidizing bacteria that fix carbon, fed by a specialized hemoglobin that transports both O$_2$ and H$_2$S simultaneously.
- **Key citation**: Arp et al. (1987), *Journal of Experimental Biology* [@Arp1987]
- **Appears in**: Chapter 7

---

***Elysia chlorotica***
- **Habitat**: Tidal marshes and shallow coastal waters of eastern North America; feeds on algae
- **Metabolism**: Steals functional chloroplasts from the alga *Vaucheria litorea* and retains them in its own digestive cells -- a temporary, non-heritable photosynthesis (kleptoplasty)
- **Claim to fame**: A living thought experiment for how chloroplast acquisition might have begun. Each generation must acquire chloroplasts anew by feeding. The chloroplasts likely persist on their own long-lived proteins and mRNAs; genome-scale analyses have not confirmed functional gene transfer to the slug (see Chapter 7).
- **Key citation**: Rumpho et al. (2008) [@Rumpho2008]
- **Appears in**: Chapter 7

[^deep-life-appe]: Li-Hung Lin et al., "Long-Term Sustainability of a High-Energy, Low-Diversity Crustal Biome," *Science* 314 (2006): 479 to 482, [doi:10.1126/science.1127376](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1127376); Dylan Chivian et al., "Environmental Genomics Reveals a Single-Species Ecosystem Deep Within Earth," *Science* 322 (2008): 275 to 278, Fig. 1 and pp. 276 to 277, [doi:10.1126/science.1155495](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1155495). The genome came from filtered fluid collected in September 2002, not a census of every fracture surface. [@Lin2006; @Chivian2008]
