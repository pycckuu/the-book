Status: planned, not executed. No students have been contacted, enrolled or tested for this module. This protocol accompanies the six accepted exercises; it does not add learner tasks or establish model validity. Execution depends on final integrated materials, editorial approval of the pilot and willing participants. EIC coordinates any later outreach.

## Participants and procedure

Plan one complete module with four to six willing graduate students or advanced undergraduates in natural sciences. Record prerequisite diagnostic responses, prior modeling experience and Python/spreadsheet route. A small convenience pilot tests usability and misunderstandings; it cannot establish a population learning effect or compare routes causally. Participation is voluntary; explain how anonymous attempts and feedback will be used and allow withdrawal. Do not collect names in the learning-analysis record.

Use the [curriculum schedule](curriculum-en.md) and the six [exercises](student-packet-en.qmd), preserving the 270 planned minutes and separately logged optional bridge. Check that participants can open the book readings, supplied data, worksheets and open answers before starting. Record any access failure as a material problem, not a conceptual error.

Learners retain their initial E01 predictions and first attempts on every exercise. Worked answers are open: record when consulted and distinguish first-attempt reasoning from corrections. Do not withhold answers or claim unaided mastery if consultation occurred. Facilitator clarification uses the existing task wording; any substantive hint is logged with exercise ID. No extra numerical run or optimizer is required. Finish with brief feedback about the existing exercises, within the follow-up allocation.

## Evidence to collect from actual attempts

For each anonymized learner ID, record route, exercise/outcome ID, first attempt, answer consultation/hints, revised attempt, rubric component scores, conceptual gates and time spent (prework/session/follow-up/bridge separately). Quote misconceptions only from actual submitted work, with a locator. Retain the actual units/support chosen for budgets, timing and residuals. Keep blank or missing work distinguishable from incorrect work.

Collect short feedback: which instruction or file was unclear, where time was spent, and what changed after checking an answer. Preserve the comment rather than replacing it with a guessed explanation. Facilitator observations must identify the attempt that supports them. Report all attempted cases, including noncompletion; missing feedback is not positive feedback.

## Predeclared success and revision criteria

These are proposed pilot decisions, not observed results:

- At least four participants supply complete attempt/consultation records. With fewer records, report a partial feasibility check rather than a successful full pilot.
- At least 75% of participants submit all six exercises and can use their chosen supplied-input route without a blocking file/access fault. Report the actual numerator and denominator; with four participants, 75% means three.
- At least 75% meet the curriculum's 70/100 and three-gate criteria after revision. Separately report how many met them before answer consultation; never present the revised proportion as unaided performance or a causal learning gain.
- Median recorded core time is at most 125% of the 270-minute plan (337.5 minutes), with bridge time separated. Report incomplete work and its time; do not drop it to make the workload look shorter.
- No unresolved critical ambiguity prevents a consistent volume/flux balance, physical/numerical spreading distinction or correct observation support. Repetition of the same misconception by two participants triggers a focused wording/example review even if scores pass.

A route blocker, failed conceptual criterion or excessive load triggers a bounded revision of the affected existing exercise/file, not automatic addition of tasks. For example, real confusion between $O(T)$ and input dose would justify clarifying the quantile label; it cannot be claimed in advance. Record the old wording, actual evidence, revision and next check. Any later repeat pilot uses willing participants and the same disclosure rules.

## Pilot report and feedback integrity

Return participant counts and exclusions, material hashes/version, route and consultation records, rubric/gate distributions, observed timings, actual misconception examples and concrete changes. Label first-attempt versus revised evidence. A before/after difference during open-answer study is descriptive; it does not establish a controlled learning effect. No result may be filled from this protocol or fabricated before attempts exist. Learner feedback can support teaching revisions, not empirical validation of the physical model.
